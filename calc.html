Graphing tips & keyboard shortcuts

Enter expressions such as y = x^2 − 4, sin(x), sqrt(x) or 2x + 1. Select an expression, then type or use the expression keypad below the graph. Buttons insert at the cursor; AC clears only the selected expression. Curves update as you edit. Up to eight curves can be shown at once. Use the coloured circles to hide or show a curve.

Drag to pan, use the + and − buttons or scroll to zoom, and pinch on a touch screen. Reset returns to the origin. With the graph focused, use the arrow keys to pan, +/− to zoom and 0 to reset.

Move over the graph or tap it to trace the curves. You can also enter an exact x value below the graph. Table shows values across the visible x range; Save PNG downloads the graph.

Supported functions include sin, cos, tan, asin, acos, atan, sqrt, abs, ln, log, exp, floor, ceil, round and sign. Trigonometry starts in radians; change to degrees when needed. Use pi or π for pi, e for Euler’s number, ^ for powers and * for multiplication.

The a slider runs from −10 to 10. Try sin(a*x) or x^2 + a. Expressions, the slider and your view are saved in this browser. Graphs show real function values; gaps appear where a function is undefined. Very rapid oscillations may need a closer zoom.

    Keyboard shortcuts & a few useful tips

    Use your keyboard or the number pad. Enter or = calculates, Backspace deletes, and Esc clears. You can select and edit any part of a calculation.

    Brackets and the usual order of operations are supported: 2 + 3 × 4 = 14. Powers come before a leading minus: −2^2 = −4. Use (−2)^2 for 4.

    For a percentage, 200 + 10% = 220, 200 − 10% = 180 and 200 × 10% = 20. A percentage on its own divides by 100.

    Scientific mode adds powers, logarithms, factorials and trigonometry. Choose DEG for degrees or RAD for radians. Close function brackets, for example sin(30). Ans uses the last calculated answer from this visit.

    Results are rounded to 14 significant digits for display. Calculations and history stay on your device.
